# The WBR, redesigned

A proposal. Built against the two goals as stated, and nothing else.

> **One team, full visibility.** Everyone understands what is happening in the
> other lines of business. Cross-functional dependencies and blockers get
> resolved in the room, so no one is left chasing things on their own.
>
> **Accountability against targets.** Where do we stand, what is working, what
> is not. I represent all of you to the board, so I am accountable too, and I
> need the same from each of you.

Five lines of business: India Sales, US Sales, Performance Marketing (India and
US), Sloosh Product & Marketing, Hiring.

---

## The diagnosis

The current format fails both goals for the same reason: **the meeting is spent
transferring information that could have been read.**

Everyone narrates their own numbers. By the time the fifth person finishes, the
first person's context is gone, so nobody actually holds the cross-business
picture — goal one fails. And because the time went on narration, blockers get
raised in the last ten minutes and leave the room unowned, which is exactly the
"chasing things on their own" problem.

The last-minute Notion updates are a symptom, not the disease. People assemble
them *because the meeting is a presentation*. Remove the presentation and the
incentive to assemble disappears.

So: **separate the reading from the meeting.**

---

## The format

**Before — the dashboard builds itself.**
Fourteen hours ahead, the system reads every team's live data and publishes a
dashboard. Rings per line of business, each one a number against its target.
Nobody updates anything. Nobody presents.

**In the room — only what needs a room.**
Numbers do not need a room. Blockers and decisions do.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **0–2 min** | **Scoreboard** | Five lines, on target or not. No narration. The founder reads it in silence. |
| **2–10 min** | **Misses only** | Only off-target metrics get spoken to. Owner answers three things: what the number is, why, and what changes this week. On-target metrics are not discussed. |
| **10–35 min** | **Blockers and dependencies** | The main event. Each blocker names who is blocked and **who can unblock it**. Resolved live, or it leaves with an owner and a date. |
| **35–50 min** | **Decisions** | Carried forward, changed, closed. One item at a time. |
| **50–60 min** | **Next meeting** | What the next review must decide, and what evidence has to exist by then. |

Twenty-five of sixty minutes on blockers. That ratio is the proposal. If
cross-functional dependencies are the stated problem, they get the majority of
the time, not the leftovers.

---

## Six rules that make it hold

**1. Nobody presents their numbers.**
They are on the dashboard before the meeting. Reading them aloud is the single
biggest consumer of the hour and it teaches nothing that a glance would not.

**2. A blocker must name who can unblock it.**
"We're blocked on engineering" is a complaint. "US Sales needs the pricing page
from Sloosh before the 30th" is a blocker. Only the second one can be resolved
in a room. The system rejects the first.

**3. Every metric has a target and a named owner.**
A number without a target cannot be accountable to anything — it is just a
number that went up or down. Metrics with no target are listed publicly on the
dashboard until someone sets one. That list going to zero is the setup process.

**4. Data comes from the system the team already uses daily.**
Not a page written on Sunday night. If a function genuinely has no daily system
behind a number, that is worth knowing — it means nobody is running that part of
the business on data, which is a bigger finding than the number itself.

**5. Freshness is visible.**
Each card shows whether its data is maintained daily or was assembled in the
hours before the meeting. Not a scold — the number may well be right. But
"assembled for this meeting" is information the room should have, and making it
visible removes the point of doing it.

**6. Nothing leaves the room unowned.**
Every blocker and every decision exits with a named owner and a date, or it is
explicitly dropped. `[OWNER NEEDED]` and `[DATE NEEDED]` print in the log until
filled. A blocker in its third week looks different from a new one, so age
cannot hide.

---

## What each person does

**Before the meeting — nothing.** That is the point. Your data is already
flowing from the system you work in.

**Once, at setup:** point the system at where your numbers live, and set your
targets. Twenty minutes, one time.

**In the room:** speak to your misses. Raise blockers with a name attached.
Take ownership of what you are given.

---

## The part that compounds

Each meeting's transcript feeds back in. Three things accumulate:

**Decisions carry forward.** The log tracks every decision as NEW, CONFIRMED,
CHANGED, REVERSED, COMPLETED, BLOCKED, DEFERRED or DROPPED. A reversal has to
say why. Nobody reconstructs September from memory in December.

**Decisions link to numbers.** Each decision points at the metrics that would
prove it worked. Next week the dashboard says whether they moved. That is the
difference between a log and a feedback loop.

**Said versus measured.** When someone states a number in the room, it gets
compared against what the data actually says. A gap raises a flag with the
quote. This is the one that earns its keep — it catches the week a team's story
starts drifting from its numbers, which is the most expensive thing a review can
miss.

---

## Holding the meeting itself accountable

Four numbers, reported monthly. Goal two applies to the process too.

- Blockers resolved in the room, against blockers carried
- Decisions with no metric attached — choices nobody is measuring
- Sources assembled last-minute rather than maintained
- Metrics still without a target

If blockers carried exceeds blockers resolved, the format is not working and
should change again.

---

## What this needs from each of the five

1. **Where your numbers live.** The sheet, board or database you already use
   daily. A link is enough.
2. **Your targets.** Weekly or monthly, for each metric that matters. Yours to
   set, and yours to be held to.
3. **Your three to five real numbers.** Not everything measurable. The ones you
   would check on a Wednesday to know whether the week is working.
4. **Your name against each one.**

Priyal will come to each of you for these. The answer to (3) is the one worth
thinking about before that conversation — the rest is mechanical.

---

## Open questions worth deciding together

- **Weekly or biweekly?** Weekly suits sales cycles and hiring. Sloosh product
  metrics may be too noisy week to week to be meaningful.
- **Does Performance Marketing report as one line or two?** It runs India and
  US. The system can show both and roll them up. The question is whether the
  room wants one accountable owner or two.
- **Who chairs the blockers section?** It needs someone willing to say "that
  leaves with an owner or it gets dropped." That is a real job and the format
  fails without it.
- **What happens when someone misses target three weeks running?** Worth
  agreeing now, in the calm, rather than in the moment.
